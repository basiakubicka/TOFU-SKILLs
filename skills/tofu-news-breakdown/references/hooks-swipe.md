# Hook swipe file — third-party news/launch breakdown

Real opening lines (first 1-2 lines) from every THIRD-PARTY post in this archetype's
golden dataset ( a golden set of real high-performing posts),
grouped by the hook pattern each one uses. **19 hooks / 11 authors, likes 852-4,262.**

**How to use this:** reference it for hook VARIETY so every draft doesn't default to
"[Company] just open-sourced X." Study the pattern, then write a fresh line for the real
event in front of you. **Do not copy any line verbatim** — these are other people's posts.
Rotate patterns across a run so consecutive posts don't share a shape.

**Credibility note (from the champion/challenger loop):** the two weakest-scoring hooks in
this file are the two that anchor on a country or a bare reaction with NO named
person/lab/company (`China just turned...`, `Impressive.`). This archetype trades on a
borrowed credential — a hook that names a real recognizable source outperforms one that
names a country or "a team." When the real event has no named actor, that is a signal the
piece may be source-capped (see `failures.md`).

---

## Pattern: named-entity-just-did-X (the workhorse — a recognizable company/org just shipped)
Line 1 names the real actor + the concrete thing they just did. Strongest when the actor is
a household-name company or a credentialed institution.

- **`Google just open-sourced their MCP server for databases.`** — paoloperrone, 980 likes (Google MCP Toolbox)
- **`Anthropic just measured which jobs AI is actually replacing.`** — a-banks, 875 likes (Anthropic Economic Index paper)
- **`Mistral just open-sourced a text-to-speech model that beats ElevenLabs`** / `3GB of RAM. Runs locally. Free:` — rubendominguezibar, 852 likes (Mistral TTS)
- **`IKEA replaced 8,500 customer service roles with AI.`** — donnellychris, 2,931 likes (IKEA retraining) — company + shock stat in one line
- **`Carnegie Mellon just open-sourced a Blackwell GPU programming book. Free.`** — paoloperrone, 1,029 likes — institution as the credential + "Free" kicker

## Pattern: breaking-alert (🚨 + "Breaking" / "just dropped" — urgency framing)
Same core as above but wrapped in a news-flash register. Note the emoji + "Breaking" combo.

- **`🚨 Breaking : Google just dropped LangExtract`** / `A Python library that pulls structured data from unstructured documents` — chorouk-malmoum, 4,262 likes (highest performer in the set)
- **`🚨 Breaking: LangChain just open-sourced Claude Code's architecture.`** / `MIT licensed. Any model. $0.` — chorouk-malmoum, 2,774 likes — line 2 stacks the license/price proof

## Pattern: named-creator / authority callout (a specific credentialed PERSON, not a company)
Anchors on an individual whose name or role carries the weight. Your own canonical top
post ("An Anthropic engineer just told us not to build agents") lives in this family.

- **`3Blue1Brown did it again. 🧠`** — eordax, 3,262 likes (Grant Sanderson video) — assumes the audience already knows the name
- **`14 minutes with an Anthropic engineer will teach you more about building agents than most devs figure out in months of trial and error.`** — eordax, 2,869 likes (Barry Zhang talk) — credential ("Anthropic engineer") + time-boxed promise

## Pattern: shock-stat lead (the number IS the hook; source named in the body)
Open on a hard, almost-unbelievable metric and dare the reader to disbelieve it.

- **`98.7% accuracy with zero vectors; this is not a typo.`** — rakeshgohel01, 2,009 likes (VectifyAI PageIndex) — "this is not a typo" pre-empts the doubt
- **`Lovable JUST raised $330M at a $6.6B valuation - 13 months after launch 🔥`** — yrebryk, 1,018 likes — funding stat + speed qualifier

## Pattern: threat / "this is scary" framing (real tool, danger angle)
Names a real tool, then frames what it can do as an alarm. Drives shares through concern.

- **`This is scary: GeoSpy AI tracks your exact location from a social media photo in 2 seconds.`** — ruben-hassid, 2,958 likes (GeoSpy)

## Pattern: capability / benefit-first (lead with what the reader can now DO, name the tool on line 2)
Delays the source name to line 2; opens on the outcome the reader gets.

- **`🚨 Your agents can now have unrestricted internet access And it's completely free !`** / `The tool is called Scrapling` — chorouk-malmoum, 993 likes

## Pattern: comparison / diss (position the new thing against an incumbent)
Frames the news as one player making another look outdated.

- **`Google's new agent framework makes LangChain look like a toy.`** — paoloperrone, 2,030 likes (Google ADK)

## Pattern: myth-vs-data setup (state the common belief, promise the data flips it)
A contrarian opener that still resolves into a named-source breakdown (here, a named report).

- **`People think AI is replacing roles and reducing headcount.`** — donnellychris, 873 likes (PwC Jobs Barometer) — the "This study shows the opposite" turn comes on the next line
- **`AI is giving companies time back. The harder question is whether anyone has a plan for it.`** — pascalbornet, 861 likes (BCG AI at Work report) — insight + the question it raises

## Pattern: reaction / visual framing (weak anchor — study as a CAUTION, not a model)
Opens on a bare reaction or "look at this," with the real actor thin or absent. These are
the lowest-credibility hooks in the set — usable only when a genuinely strong named source
lands immediately after. **Prefer a named-entity hook when you have one.**

- **`This is basically the entire tech world in one picture 👏😂`** — linasbeliunas, 2,115 likes (Apple/Google payments) — carried by the specific $1B / $20B numbers that follow, not the hook
- **`China just turned 10,000 drones into a single coordinated system 🤯`** — pascalbornet, 2,243 likes — anchors on a country, no named lab/company (scored weakest on source credibility in the loop)
- **`Impressive.`** — pascalbornet, 1,988 likes (Finland waste-heat) — one-word reaction, no named source at all (source-capped)
