# Resource Roundup Hook Template Bank

10 hook templates extracted from the golden dataset (23 real, high-performing
multi-item free-list posts, sheet "2 Resource roundup" in
a golden-dataset spreadsheet). Each is a **distinct
rhetorical mechanism** with **fixed, literal words** locking the rhythm in place, not a
vibe description or a reskin of the same sentence. Pick the template that fits the
authority you're borrowing. Don't default to template 1 just because it's first.

**Format rule for every slot:** the bracket itself must carry the specific description,
never a generic label like `[SLOT A]`. Write `[the recognized institution whose name
stops the scroll on its own]`, not `[SLOT A]` with the description in a separate
paragraph below. A drafter should be able to fill the slot correctly from the bracket
text alone, without needing to cross-reference anything else.

Every template has:
- **Fixed template**: the literal words, verbatim, with descriptive `[bracketed]` slots
  inline. Read the fixed words alone (skip the brackets) and they must form a
  recognizable, repeatable rhythm on their own.
- **Slot test**: for slots needing more than the bracket can hold, a concrete pass/fail
  check (not additional vague description, a real test you can apply).
- **Why it works**: the psychological/rhetorical mechanism, secondary to the fixed words.
- **Real example**: the actual post the template was extracted from.
- **Use when / non-negotiables**: situational fit, plus any hard safety rule.

**The universal rule underneath all 10:** this archetype borrows authority. Every hook
below gets its scroll-stop from one of three sources: a recognized institution's name
(Stanford, Harvard, MIT, Anthropic, NVIDIA, Google), a real price being zeroed out
("$10,000", "$500 courses"), or the poster's own verifiable effort ("I spent 1000
hours"). A hook with none of the three has no engine. 69% of overperforming TOFU posts
name a recognized entity; 41% name it in line 1.

### 1. Priced-Thing-Made-Free
**Fixed template:** "[The recognized institution or company whose name stops the scroll on its own] just made [$X,000 of / the $X,000] [the category of education or resource being zeroed out] free."
**Slot test for the price claim:** the dollar figure must be a real, defensible market
comparison, what comparable paid education actually costs (a bootcamp, a certificate
program). If you cannot point at a real $10K product this list replaces, use the
count-based variant (template 2) instead. Never inflate the number to make the hook land.
**Why it works:** three authority sources in one line: the name, the price, and the
word "free". The price anchors the value; "just" makes it news; "free" removes the last
objection. The reader's next question ("which courses?") can only be answered by expanding.
**Real example:** *"Anthropic just made $10,000 of AI education free."* (you, 1,675
likes / 358 shares / share-like 0.214, the strongest roundup in the golden set)

### 2. Institution Dropped the Whole Thing
**Fixed template:** "[The recognized institution] just dropped [their entire curriculum / N free courses / N free lectures on the named topic]."
**Slot test for the scale phrase:** "entire" and the N must be literally true. Count
the items before writing the number. If it's 7 courses, the hook says 7. A rounded or
invented count fails the whole post's truth gate, not just the hook.
**Why it works:** the surprise is totality. One free course is ordinary; an ENTIRE
curriculum dropped at once reads as an event. "Dropped" carries release-energy that
"released" or "offers" doesn't.
**Real example:** *"Stanford just dropped their entire AI curriculum."* (you, 1,055
likes / 187 shares) · variant with a count: *"MIT just released ten free AI courses for
leaders."* (Gabriel Millien, 1,153 likes / 142 shares)

---

### 3. Killed-the-Industry Inversion
**Fixed template:** "[The recognized institution] just [killed / made obsolete / made irrelevant] [the industry or product category that just lost its reason to charge]."
**Fixed two-line form:** "[Institution] just made / every $[X]K [category] course irrelevant."
**Slot test for the displaced category:** name the thing people actually pay for today
(paid AI courses, bootcamps, certifications), not an abstraction. The hook works because
a real market just got undercut; if no one was paying for it, there was nothing to kill.
**Why it works:** it's the free-vs-expensive inversion at maximum stakes. Not "this is
now free" but "everything you were paying for just lost its justification." The reader
who paid feels it; the reader who didn't feels lucky. Both expand.
**Real example:** *"Harvard just made / every $10K AI course irrelevant."* (Gabriel
Millien, 1,356 likes / 265 shares / share-like 0.195) · *"MIT just killed the AI course
industry. (Bookmark this 👇)"* (you, 688 likes / 109 shares. Note the parenthetical
save-bait inside the hook; optional, and count it against line length)

### 4. Quiet-Drop
**Fixed template:** "[The recognized company] quietly dropped [N free courses / resources on the named topic]."
**Slot test for "quietly":** only use when the release genuinely was unannounced or
under-promoted, no launch event, no press cycle. If the company ran a launch campaign,
"quietly" is false and a reader who saw the launch will say so in the comments.
**Why it works:** "quietly" implies insider knowledge, the poster caught something the
crowd missed. The reader reshares to be the person who surfaces hidden things, which is
exactly the reshare psychology this archetype runs on.
**Real example:** *"Anthropic quietly dropped 13 free Claude courses."* (Adam Danyal,
2,123 likes / 359 shares) · *"Google quietly released 7 free AI courses."* (you, 67
likes / 2 shares, the cautionary case: quiet-drop hooks still need stakes and a reframe
in the body; the hook alone doesn't carry a list with no meaning)
**Use when:** the resources are real and genuinely under-the-radar. Pair it in the body
with the stakes the quiet release displaces, or the hook's whisper becomes the post's
volume too.

---

### 5. Stop-Paying Imperative
**Fixed template:** "Stop [paying for / buying] [the expensive thing with a real price tag: "$500 courses," "AI engineering bootcamps"]."
**Slot test for the expensive thing:** must be something the audience is visibly paying
for right now, at a real price point. "Stop wasting money on education" fails (vague);
"Stop buying expensive $500 courses to learn AI" passes (named behavior, named price).
**Why it works:** the imperative creates an open loop, if not that, then what?, that
only the list can close. It also flatters the reader: you're about to be let in on what
the people paying $500 don't know.
**Real example:** *"Stop paying for AI engineering bootcamps."* (Paolo Perrone, 1,653
likes / 259 shares) · *"Stop buying expensive $500 courses to learn AI."* (Ruben Hassid,
4,727 likes / 667 shares)
**Non-negotiable:** the list that follows must actually replace the paid thing, item for
item in spirit. If the free alternatives are weaker, the hook overpromised.

### 6. Effort-Flex Distillation
**Fixed template:** "I spent [N hours / N years, a real, verifiable amount of time] [learning / figuring out] [the hard skill]. / [These N free resources actually deliver. / Here are the guides I wish someone had sent me earlier.]"
**Slot test for the time claim:** the number must be real and yours (or the poster's).
Never rounded up, never invented to compete with someone else's number. 100 real hours
beats a fabricated 1000. If the time wasn't spent, the whole template is unavailable;
pick another.
**Why it works:** the poster's own effort replaces the institution as the borrowed
authority. "I spent 3 years so you don't have to" is asymmetric effort perception in one
line; the reader gets the distillation in 30 seconds. The "wish someone had sent me"
frame adds loss aversion.
**Real example:** *"I spent 1000 hours figuring out Claude the hard way. / Here are
guides I wish someone had sent me earlier:"* (Ruben Hassid, 5,449 likes / 613 shares,
the most-liked post in the golden set) · *"I spent 3 years learning LLM inference. /
These are the only resources worth your time."* (Paolo Perrone, 1,684 likes / 208
shares) · Your version: *"I spent 100h+ learning AI and Agentic Coding. / These 8
free Anthropic courses actually deliver."* (303 likes / 40 shares)
**Use when:** you have genuinely put in the hours on the topic and can defend the
number. This is the only template where the poster IS the authority. Use it when no
single institution anchors the list.

### 7. Everyone's-Paying Flip
**Fixed template:** "Everyone's paying for [the thing with a real price tag]. / [The recognized institution] just made that [pointless / free / obsolete]."
**Slot test for line 1:** "everyone" must be defensible, the paid behavior must be
visibly common in the audience right now (people posting about bootcamps, course ads in
the feed). Per the shared verification rule: if you can't point at the paying crowd,
don't claim it.
**Why it works:** two-line whiplash. Line 1 describes the reader's world, line 2
invalidates it. The word "pointless" is the flip; it converts the reader's recent
spending (or near-spending) into urgency to see the free replacement.
**Real example:** *"Everyone's paying for AI courses. / NVIDIA just made that pointless."*
(Gabriel Millien, 1,416 likes / 225 shares)
**Non-negotiable:** line 2 names the institution. "Free alternatives exist" without the
name is a claim; "NVIDIA just made that pointless" is an event.

### 8. News-Event Launch
**Fixed template:** "[Optional 🚨 Breaking:] [The recognized institution] just launched [N FREE courses / resources] with [the concrete sweetener: certificates, no paywall, no sign-up]."
**Slot test for "Breaking":** the release must be days old, not months. A three-month-old
list is not breaking news and the emoji will read as crying wolf. Check the actual
release date before the 🚨 goes in.
**Why it works:** it borrows the news archetype's dated-event urgency and spends it on
a resource drop. The sweetener (certificates, no sign-up) answers the reader's first
skeptical question, "what's the catch?", inside the hook itself.
**Real example:** *"🚨 Breaking: Anthropic just launched 13 FREE AI courses with
certificates"* (Chorouk Malmoum, 1,900 likes / 350 shares / share-like 0.184)
**Use when:** the drop is genuinely fresh (within ~1-2 weeks). Past that window, use
templates 1-4, which don't decay.

### 9. Found-It-For-You Completeness
**Fixed template:** "I found every [resource / course / guide] you need to [the concrete, named outcome: "become a Claude Certified Architect," not "get better at AI"]. / (Save it)"
**Slot test for the outcome:** must be a specific, nameable destination the reader could
put on a resume or a roadmap: a certification, a job title, a shipped capability. "To
level up" fails (vague); "to become a Claude Certified Architect" passes (a real
credential with an exam).
**Why it works:** "every" is the completeness promise. The reader will never need
another list for this goal, which is the strongest possible save trigger. The
parenthetical "(Save it)" states the intended behavior outright, and this archetype is
judged on exactly that behavior.
**Real example:** *"I found every resource you need to become a Claude Certified
Architect / (Save it)"* (Chorouk Malmoum, 4,006 likes / 571 shares)
**Non-negotiable:** the list must actually be complete for the stated outcome, the full
path, not a sample. If gaps exist, narrow the outcome until the list covers it.

### 10. Best-of Named Series
**Fixed template:** "These [N] [lectures / courses] from [the recognized institution] are the BEST for anyone wanting to [the specific depth outcome: "learn and understand LLMs in depth"]."
**Slot test for "BEST":** a superlative is a claim the poster must be willing to defend
in the comments. Use it when the list is genuinely the strongest available set for the
stated outcome (a famous lecture series, a canonical course). If it's merely good, drop
"BEST". "Worth your time" is the honest downgrade and still works.
**Why it works:** the institution plus the superlative pre-answers "why this list and
not another one." The depth outcome ("in depth," "from zero to deployed") filters the
audience; the right readers self-select in, which concentrates saves among exactly the
people who'll use the list.
**Real example:** *"These 9 lectures from Stanford University are the BEST for anyone
wanting to learn and understand LLMs in depth"* (Chorouk Malmoum, 2,222 likes / 233
shares)
**Use when:** the items share one source (one course series, one lecture playlist) rather
than many. For multi-source lists, templates 1-6 fit better.

## Explicitly excluded from this bank
Templates that only appeared once with weak reusability, or that overlapped too closely
with a stronger template above, are intentionally left out rather than padded in to hit a
round number. Notable exclusions:
- **Bare recommendation** ("Free Claude learning videos from Anthropic + 1 live
  workshop", Alex Wang, 5,739 likes): it worked on the strength of Alex Wang's existing
  audience, not the hook mechanism; there is no inversion, no stakes, and nothing to
  teach a drafter. Excluded as non-replicable.
- **Count-tease without authority** ("26 Claude Code shortcuts that most users don't know
  exist"): that's a tactical how-to hook wearing a list; it belongs to a different
  archetype's bank.

## Verifying the authority claim (never assume it)
Every template above borrows authority from a name, a price, or real effort. Verify
before writing, in order of rigor:
1. **The institution actually released it, and it's actually free.** Open the real page.
   A waitlist, a trial, or a paywalled "free" course kills the post's truth gate, and
   this archetype trades on nothing else.
2. **The price comparison is real.** "$10,000 of education" means a comparable paid
   product exists at that price. If you can't name it, soften to the count variant.
3. **The effort number is yours.** "I spent 100h+" requires your real hours. Ask, don't
   round.
4. **The release is fresh enough for the template.** Breaking = days. Quietly = genuinely
   unannounced. Just = recent. If the drop is months old and widely covered, the honest
   hook is the effort-flex (6) or best-of (10), not fake news-energy.

## How to use this bank when drafting
1. Identify the authority source: is there ONE recognized institution (→ 1, 2, 3, 4, 8),
   a real price being displaced (→ 1, 3, 5, 7), your own verified effort (→ 6), a
   complete-path outcome (→ 9), or one canonical series (→ 10)? Match the template to the
   authority you actually have. Don't force template 1 by default.
2. Fill every bracket with something **specific and real**: a named institution, a real
   count, a defensible price, a verifiable time claim. If a slot can't be filled with
   something real, don't fabricate it. Pick a different template or ask for the
   missing specific.
3. Check the "why it works" line. If your draft hook doesn't actually create that
   mechanism (the inversion, the completeness promise, the insider catch), it's not using
   the template correctly even if it superficially matches the pattern.
4. **Run the slot-substitution test before finalizing:** read only the fixed words (skip
   the brackets). Do they form a recognizable rhythm on their own, with no room for
   creative reinterpretation? If a bracket only says something generic like `[SLOT A]`
   with no description inline, it's not filled in correctly. Go back and write the
   specific description directly into the bracket.
5. **Count the characters.** Line 1 ≤ 62, line 2 ≤ 50, no exceptions. The save-bait
   parenthetical in template 3 counts toward the limit.
