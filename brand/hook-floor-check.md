# Hook Floor-Check — cross-creator structural findings

**Provenance note (read this first):** unlike the rest of the voice materials, this file
is NOT about any one person's own voice. It's a cross-creator structural finding pulled
from a 556-post sample of the general LinkedIn AI-content corpus — any author, tofu-tagged,
low/mid/high x-factor tiers, tagged for loop structure, stance, template, register. Every
real example quoted below is someone else's post, used as calibration for what a strong
or weak hook mechanic looks like — never as your voice or something to copy directly. Run
this alongside, never instead of, your own voice rules in `linkedin-voice.md`.

Referenced by `linkedin-voice.md` and by the TOFU/MOFU skills that don't route
through that file. Run this AFTER a skill's own hard-limit hook rules (char counts,
archetype-specific patterns), as a floor check before finalizing any hook.

---

## What this is and isn't

Structural hook mechanics reliably separate BROKEN hooks from FUNCTIONAL ones (a
real, large gap), but do NOT reliably separate FUNCTIONAL from EXCELLENT (near-zero
difference between mid and high performers on every structural tag tested). Two
candidate differentiators (confrontational "direct_challenge" phrasing, "spicy"
controversy) looked promising pooled across types but evaporated once split by TOFU
type — a mix artifact, not a real effect. Don't re-chase either as a general rule.

**The conclusion that matters:** whether a hook lands emotionally depends on the
topic/take being genuinely charged for the audience right now (timing, live
sentiment, a real nerve) — that lives outside the post text and can't be engineered
from mechanics alone. **A hook checklist's job is not to predict virality. It's to
not waste a good take by executing it weakly.** Picking a topic with real emotional
charge is a human judgment call, not something this checklist can supply.

---

## The checklist

Run this on every hook + rehook (line 1 through the fold, ~lines 2-5) before
finalizing:

1. **Does it open a real loop?** An explicit unresolved question, withheld outcome, or
   previewed-but-not-delivered list/count — not just vague intrigue. (Low performers
   open a loop 39% of the time; functional ones do it ~68% of the time — this is the
   single most load-bearing check.)

   **Sub-check that's easy to miss: previewing a COUNT is not the same as withholding
   the OUTCOME.** "7 settings turn it into a system that writes like you, every time"
   previews a count (7) but ALSO states the payoff in full — nothing is left unknown
   except which 7 items, a weak, mechanical gap. Compare: "7 settings later, mine
   never has to be told twice" — the count is previewed but the mechanism/outcome
   ("never told twice" — how? why?) stays genuinely unresolved. Test: after reading
   the hook, can the reader already state what the post proves/delivers in their own
   words? If yes, the loop is closed even if a count or list was previewed — rewrite
   so the RESULT, not just the item contents, is what's missing.

   **Where the count actually lands (checked against 52 list_lead posts): line 1 in
   ~46% of cases, line 2 in ~33%, neither in ~19% — line 1 is the single most common
   spot but it's not a rule, and both patterns work equally well.** The real logic:
   the count appears wherever the list itself starts. If line 1 IS the list-preview
   ("Top 9 free courses to learn AI", "These 16 GitHub repos are better than any
   $1,000 course"), the number is in line 1. If line 1 is a setup/tease with no
   number ("Your success in life will boil down to your ability to:", "You don't
   need 100 tutorials.") and the list itself begins in line 2, the number lands
   there instead. Both shapes hit high tier in this sample — don't force the count
   into line 1 if a setup line reads stronger there; just make sure the count shows
   up in whichever line starts the actual list.

   *Strong, real example (Conceptual Explainer, real example):*
   > "Most people think Agentic AI is just 'ChatGPT + tools' / They're wrong. / This
   > diagram shows the full stack. / Five layers. Each one matters."
   The loop (what are the five layers?) is opened and still unanswered at the fold —
   the reader has to expand to find out.

   *Weak, real example (same archetype):*
   > "This video introduces the vital concept of 'global citizenship,' urging a shift
   > in how we perceive our roles in an interconnected world."
   No question is raised, nothing is withheld — the whole point is stated flat in
   line 1. Nothing pulls the reader further.

2. **Is the loop still open at the end of the fold?** A loop fully answered by line
   4-5 is a wasted setup — the reader has no reason to keep scrolling.

3. **Is it concrete, not abstract?** A real name, number, scene, or specific claim
   beats a general concept every time the topic allows it. "Sentiment," "impact," and
   "circumspect" are abstraction-tells — swap for the specific thing they stand in for.

   *Strong, real example (News Breakdown, real example):*
   > "My biggest takeaways from Boris Cherny (Head of Claude Code): / 1. Coding is now
   > 'solved' for most use cases. Boris hasn't written a single line of code by hand
   > since November..."
   Named person, named role, a checkable specific ("hasn't written code since
   November") — not "a leading expert says AI is changing how we code."

   *Weak, real example (Contrarian):*
   > "Artificial intelligence is transforming entertainment by enhancing creativity
   > rather than replacing it. AI tools assist filmmakers, writers, and game..."
   Generic nouns ("transforming," "enhancing creativity"), no named person, no number,
   no scene — could describe any AI post ever written.

4. **Does it use direct address where the archetype calls for it?** Casual,
   register-appropriate "you/your" tracks with functional hooks in most archetypes —
   check the per-archetype baseline below; forcing it where a type doesn't support it
   (e.g. News Breakdown, which runs more concrete/third-person) reads off.

5. **Is the register right for the archetype?** Don't default to essayistic long
   sentences unless the archetype/topic genuinely calls for it — casual, punchy wins
   the majority baseline across every archetype tested.

**This checklist does NOT tell you if the take itself is good.** If the take is flat,
no amount of loop-tightening fixes it; if the take is genuinely charged, this
checklist keeps the execution from wasting it.

## Per-archetype baselines (don't apply one universal bar)

| Archetype | opens_loop baseline | takes_a_side baseline | abstraction baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contrarian | ~65% | ~84% | ~86% abstract |
| Conceptual Explainer | ~82% | ~73% | ~84% abstract |
| News Breakdown | ~57% | ~29% | ~84% CONCRETE |
| Resource Roundup | ~94% | ~30% | ~70% concrete |
| Relatable Mirror | ~23% (loops are NOT the norm) | ~56% | ~97% abstract |

A Relatable Mirror hook without an open loop is normal, not weak — don't force one.
A Resource Roundup hook without an open loop is unusual and worth a second look.

**MOFU is NOT covered by this table.** MOFU's curiosity mechanic is structurally
different (personal-stakes curiosity, not belief-correction) — see your MOFU skill's
own floor-check section, which uses a separate, qualitative (not statistically-tiered)
set of real examples.

## Hook TEMPLATE reliability — pick from the safe tier first, treat the rest as a gamble

"Opens a loop" tells you a template CAN work. It doesn't tell you how often it
actually pays off versus flops. Pulled from the same 556-post TOFU sample, template
tagged per hook, tier distribution shown as % of that template's total uses (n≥15
only — smaller counts excluded as unreliable):

| Template | High % | Low % | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| direct_challenge | 46.5% | 2.3% | **reliable** |
| most_people_think_X | 45.7% | 3.2% | **reliable** |
| bare_claim | 42.4% | 5.9% | **reliable** |
| number_stat_lead | 38.9% | 0.0% | **reliable** |
| personal_confession | 35.3% | 5.9% | reliable, thinner sample (n=17) |
| list_lead | 40.4% | 5.8% | **reliable** |
| question_lead | 20.0% | 26.7% | **GAMBLE — near coin-flip, avoid as default** |

**How to use this:** when a topic could support more than one template shape, default
to the reliable tier (direct_challenge, most_people_think_X, bare_claim,
number_stat_lead, list_lead) — all of them land in the high tier roughly 40-47% of
the time they're used, against a low-tier rate under 6%. `question_lead` succeeds and
fails at almost the same rate (20% vs 27%) — it is not a safer, softer choice, it's
higher variance. Only reach for it when the topic itself is a genuine open question
nothing else fits (and even then, know you're taking a bigger swing, not a safe bet).

**This is about template SHAPE, not about opening a loop correctly** — a
`most_people_think_X` hook can still fail check #1 above if it states the outcome
along with the claim (see the sub-check on count-vs-outcome). Template reliability
and loop-quality are two separate checks; run both.

## Body checks (run on the FULL post, not just the hook)

These are separate from the hook checks above — they apply to the whole draft.

### Reading level — data-validated
Computed via Flesch-Kincaid grade level across 856 tagged TOFU+MOFU posts (stdlib
implementation over internal tagged data):

| | Low performers | Mid | High |
|---|---|---|---|
| TOFU median grade | 10.1 | 5.8 | 6.2 |
| MOFU median grade | 8.0 | 6.5 | **5.7** |

Low performers consistently write at a harder reading level (grade 8-10) than
mid/high performers (grade 6, roughly 8th-grade comprehension or below). This holds
in both funnels and MOFU shows a clean drop all the way to the top tier. **Target:
grade 6-8.** If a draft scores above grade 8, look for long sentences, nested
clauses, or multi-syllable words with a shorter common synonym, and simplify.

### Single point — now data-validated (corrected methodology)
**Every post must argue, teach, or tell exactly ONE point.** My first attempt to test
this was flawed: a tagger that conflated "has many list items" with "has many
points" flagged a 25-item prompting list as "25 points" when it's one point (level
up your prompting) with 25 supporting facets. Re-ran with a corrected test on 190
posts (95 low-tier, 95 high-tier, TOFU+MOFU): **single_point is now defined as "can
you summarize the post's payload in one sentence with no 'and' joining two
unrelated ideas" — a list/numbered structure serving ONE thesis still counts as
single_point=true, no matter how many items.**

Result: ~94-96% of ALL posts (low and high alike) pass this test — it's a near-
universal floor, not a strong tier differentiator on its own. But the 11 posts (of
190) that failed it are genuinely instructive about what "two points" looks like in
practice — a real topic plus an unrelated tacked-on plug, or a real thread that
drifts into an unconnected tangent:

*Real failure example (real example, despite the flaw — this is a case where
other strengths compensated, not proof the flaw doesn't matter):*
> Market-share data on OpenAI losing ground to Gemini and Grok, AND separately the
> author plugs Claude Opus 4.5 as their personal go-to model. Two unconnected claims
> sharing one post.

*Real failure example (real example, same caveat):*
> Eight loosely-connected takeaways from AI lab meetings — solo-practitioner
> strategy, SF vs NYC geography, an underserved customer cohort, shipping speed,
> AND an unrelated observation about people not wearing tech swag anymore. A grab-bag,
> not one point.

**Test to apply:** can the post's payload be stated in one sentence with no "and"
joining two different ideas? If the "and" is doing real work joining two ideas
(rather than just listing two supporting facts for one idea), it's two posts
wearing one post's clothes. Cut one.

### No filler — now data-validated (corrected methodology)
**Every line must move the post forward** — no throat-clearing, no restating the
same idea twice, no hedge that adds nothing. Same corrected 190-post pass: filler
was also near-universally low (median 5-9% of lines), meaning even strong posts
carry a SMALL amount of connective tissue — this is a floor rule (don't let filler
creep above ~15-20% of lines), not something that needs to hit exactly zero.

**What real "acceptable, tiny" filler looks like** (from actual high performers,
correctly NOT flagged as a problem at their low ratios):
> "That's the whole talk in one sentence." (real example) — a light
> transitional beat, not padding.
> "No toxic positivity needed." (real example) — a brief aside that
> still carries tone, not dead air.

**Test to apply:** could this specific line be deleted without losing new
information, a new detail, or forward momentum in the argument/story? A concrete
example, a specific number, or a vivid detail that supports the ONE point is NEVER
filler, no matter how many of them there are — filler is specifically the connective
tissue between real content, and a small amount of it is normal and fine.

## How attention is actually held (structure, by post FAMILY — not one universal shape)

Word-count/list-count metrics miss the real mechanism: a resource roundup holds
attention through scannable value density, a personal story holds it through
escalation and a turning point, an argument holds it through mounting proof. These
are genuinely different shapes — don't apply one universal "story arc" template to
every post type. Tested on 190 posts (95 low / 95 high, TOFU+MOFU), grouped into 5
structural families by archetype, each scored on its OWN attention mechanic.

### STORY family (MOFU personal narratives — n=75, solid sample)
| Check | Low | High | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Has real escalation (stakes rise before resolution) | 27% | 40% | +13pp |
| Has a named turning point | 30% | 33% | flat |
| Turning point SHOWN not TOLD | 27% | 27% | flat |
| Reaches a resolution | 90% | 93% | flat |

**What this means:** most story posts (low and high alike) already have a turning
point and a resolution — those are table stakes, not what separates the top. The one
real lever is **escalation** — do the stakes/tension genuinely build before the pivot,
or does the post jump straight from setup to lesson with nothing rising in between.
A story that names a failure but doesn't let it get WORSE before the turn reads flat
even with a real turning point present.

### ARGUMENT family (Contrarian, Surprising Number, MOFU opinion-stance — n=38)
| Check | Low | High | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claim stated in first 2-3 lines | 79% | 93% | +14pp |
| Proof escalates (each point adds new weight) | 38% | 57% | **+20pp** |
| Has a reversal/twist | 54% | 50% | flat |
| Lands on a sharp punchline close | 38% | **79%** | **+41pp** |

**What this means: this is the strongest, cleanest signal in the whole structural
analysis.** Ending on a sharp, memorable restatement of the claim — not trailing off,
not restating blandly — is the single biggest lever tested anywhere in this file
(+41pp). Second: the proof has to escalate — the second and third supporting points
must add NEW weight to the claim, not just restate the first point with different
words. A flat list of equally-weighted "also true" facts under a strong hook still
underperforms a post that builds its case.

### TEACH family (Conceptual Explainer, How-To, News Breakdown — n=40)
| Check | Low | High | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steps genuinely build on each other (order matters) | 26% | 41% | +15pp |
| Has a clear "now it clicks" payoff/synthesis moment | 57% | **77%** | **+20pp** |
| Difficulty curve | 57% flat / 35% building | 47% building / 29% flat | shifts toward "building" |

**What this means:** a teaching post that reads as a genuinely ordered sequence
(step 2 requires step 1 to land) beats one where the "steps" could be shuffled with
no loss — and having an explicit synthesis line that ties the pieces together into
one point (the recap-quadruplet move already in several skills) is the second real
lever.

### LIST family (Resource Roundup, Meme — n=24, THIN, directional only)
| Check | Low | High | delta |
|---|---|---|---|
| Items are genuinely distinct | 42% | 50% | +8pp (weak) |
| Value density stays consistent (doesn't thin out) | 42% | 50% | +8pp (weak) |
| Has deliberate ordering logic | 42% | 33% | −8pp (noisy, likely n too small) |

**Honest caveat:** n=12 per tier is too thin to trust the direction confidently —
treat as a hypothesis to retest, not a rule. The one thing worth flagging anyway: a
roundup where item 5 clearly has less real substance behind it than item 1 (the
poster ran out of material and padded to hit a count) is a real, visible failure
mode even in this small sample.

### REFRAME family (Relatable Mirror — n=13, TOO THIN, do not treat as validated)
Only 1 low-tier post in the entire sample — this family is not testable with the
current data. Do not draw conclusions from the numbers; more low-tier Relatable
Mirror posts need tagging before this family says anything trustworthy.
